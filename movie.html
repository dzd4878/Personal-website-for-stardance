How the Moon Fell from the Sky and No One Even Noticed

★★★★⯨

This movie is not what you would call a miracle comedown from the moon to enlighten us all; the movie is vague, overly symbolic, and dense (like Benji). The actors are good but too "human"; you can feel that in each of their words they carry something. Each movement is like a knife being stabbed in the heart of a young man, from imagining and dreaming to the brutal realization, but what is most impressive is that he doesn't cry or scream, but he just tries to hang on not to a "promised" love but to what he had, to the attention that his best friend used to give him and the part of his life he shared. He doesn't brutalize himself out of pleasure; he doesn't try to break what they had. He is left alone and remains that way just for the benefit of his so-called best friend, but when his story gets corrupted by the same girl who stole from him what he cherished the most, he breaks. He is forced to play the role he attributed to the one he loved for so long not as a path to self-acceptance but almost as a comedy of what he felt and what he was. "Oh my God, you are so dense. It's about me. I am Scarlet; you are Ash. "I'm in love with you; I've been in love with you for as long as i can remember," he said But benji only responds with "We are bestfriend that's it." He didn't realize that Ruben could live with this forever, unrequited love, but not without his best friend. Benji had forgotten about his best friend and his struggle for a girl.
